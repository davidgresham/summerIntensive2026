About the Program

Program Overview

The Gresham Lab Summer Research Intensive is a five-week, full-day research experience at the NYU Department of Biology. Students were immersed in the research environment from 9 am to 5 pm each day, working alongside Professor David Gresham to pursue active research questions in yeast genetics, molecular biology, genomics, and computational biology.

Through project-based learning, students gained hands-on experience with fundamental methods spanning molecular biology, genetics, microbiology, genomics, statistical analysis, computer programming, and bioinformatics. By the end of the program each student was able to work confidently at the bench and to move fluidly between the acquisition of experimental data and its computational analysis.

Assessment was based on participation, maintenance of a laboratory notebook, a final oral presentation, and the construction of a personal website summarizing experimental findings.

Why Experiential Learning?

Modern biology demands fluency in both the laboratory and on the computer. Traditional courses tend to teach these as separate disciplines, but real research requires integrating them seamlessly — designing an experiment, collecting data, writing code to analyze it, and interpreting the result, often within the same afternoon.

This intensive bootcamp format places students directly inside that cycle from day one. By working on open-ended, real research questions rather than pre-designed exercises, students develop the problem-solving skills, resilience, and scientific intuition that can only come from authentic research experience. Each day’s experimental results shaped the next day’s plan, mirroring the dynamic and iterative nature of scientific inquiry.

The program also bridges the widening gap between bench science and data science, equipping students with statistical reasoning, programming fluency in R, and bioinformatics skills alongside traditional laboratory techniques.

Research Projects

Students pursued three active research projects in the Gresham Lab, progressing from fundamental techniques to independent experimental design over the five weeks.

Project 1: Yeast Display Systems

Generation and testing of yeast-display systems using fluorescent proteins (mCitrine and mCherry). Each student designed and built a unique construct combining the AGA2 surface anchor, a single-chain antibody fragment (scFv), and a fluorescent reporter in a different configuration, assembled using NEB HiFi DNA Assembly.

Constructs: AGA2-scFv-mCherry • AGA2-mCherry-scFv • AGA2-scFv-mCitrine • AGA2-mCitrine-scFv • AGA2-mCitrine • AGA2-mCherry

HiFi Assembly Fluorescent Proteins Yeast Display
Project 2: CNV Reporter Strains

Construction of copy-number variation (CNV) reporter strains at the ERG11 and GAP1 loci. Students tested whether CRISPR/Cas9-mediated cutting of a kanMX selection cassette increases the efficiency of chromosomal integration in multiple yeast deletion backgrounds, with and without the CRISPR system.

Strains: ERG11::mCitrine CNV reporter • GAP1 CNV reporter • Four gene-deletion backgrounds

CRISPR/Cas9 CNV Reporters Yeast Transformation
Project 3: Whole Genome Resequencing

Preparation and computational analysis of whole genome resequencing data from Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Students learned to align sequencing reads to a reference genome using bwa, call variants using GATK, and analyze differential expression using DESeq2 in R.

Pipeline: Library prep → sequencing → alignment → variant calling → statistical analysis

WGS bwa • GATK DESeq2
